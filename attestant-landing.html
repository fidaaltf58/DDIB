The problem

Three parties fail at the same moment, for the same reason.

The learning is real. The evidence of it is stranded — sitting in a PDF, a platform profile, an HR system — held by everyone except the person who earned it.

The one rule

Whatever confers access cannot be bought — whatever can be bought confers nothing.

Architecture

Built on Hedera. Trust doesn't route through us.

Two services do the work; a registry, not an operator, is what verification actually trusts.

HTS · Hedera Token Service

Issues the tokens

Issuers mint their own XP and Reward Tokens. Freeze-after-mint enforces non-transferability on soulbound assets; wipe keys let issuers reclaim unspent reserves at expiry.

HCS · Hedera Consensus Service

Publishes the credentials

Credentials are published in full — no links, nothing to break when a certificate moves. Redemption burns tokens and releases reserves atomically, or not at all.

The governing stance

Per-issuer sovereignty

No authority spans issuers, and no party holds authority the Operator is explicitly forbidden from holding. XP doesn't aggregate across issuers. Freeze keys stay with the issuer that minted the token. Governance runs one DAO per issuer, weighted by that issuer's own soulbound XP — so influence can't be bought and can't be borrowed from somewhere else.

onboarding
Operator creates the candidate's Hedera account at signup and hands the key over non-custodially — no wallet install required to start.
signing
Employment events are propose → accept: organisation proposes, candidate co-signs within a window, silence lets it lapse.
reserves
Pools stay Rand-native. Conversion happens once, when a funder capitalises a pool — not on every redemption.
verification
Issuer keys resolve via a public HCS registry through mirror nodes — never an Operator-hosted API.
revocation
Fraud or issuer error only. Nothing is deleted — a revocation is a new record, and verification always checks for one.
